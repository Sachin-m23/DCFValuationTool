Verdict
Where the value sits
Left: a waterfall from the PV of each forecast year's FCFF through the terminal value to enterprise value, then across the net-debt bridge to equity value — each step is labelled with its share of EV, so a model hiding everything in the terminal bar is obvious at a glance. Right: today's price compounded to the DCF value per share; the solid line is the current inputs, the dashed lines and shaded band are the bear and bull cases around them (±3pp growth, ±2pp margin, ∓0.5pp ERP), and the dotted line is today's price — green paths end above it, red below.
Method: drivers are ratios of revenue calibrated to 5y history — levels AND trends: margin expansion/contraction and rising/falling capex intensity are carried forward (damped, capped) rather than averaged away, and Yr-1 growth blends last-year momentum with the 3y CAGR; explicit-year revenue growth fades Yr-1 → Yr-N, where Yr-N is set from the company's own trajectory (it keeps roughly 55% of its excess growth over the terminal rate — a 30% compounder decays to ~17%, not to 2.5%), while the perpetuity uses the separate terminal growth rate (which must stay below WACC — a Gordon model cannot take a growth rate above the discount rate); FCFF = EBIT×(1−t) + D&A − capex − ΔNWC; discounting is selectable (mid-year: Yr t at t−0.5 and TV at N−0.5; year-end: Yr t at t and TV at N — the TV exponent always matches the yearly convention); terminal capex converges to D&A + g×(net PP&E/revenue) so the perpetuity only carries growth-sustaining reinvestment; Gordon-growth terminal cross-checked as an implied exit EV/EBITDA multiple against today's market multiple; equity bridge uses net debt. Educational model, not investment advice.
